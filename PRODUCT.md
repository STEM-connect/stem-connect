# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Two audiences, one site:

- **Candidates** — senior/experienced tech professionals in Canada (Product, Design, Engineering, Data, Go-to-Market). Often passive, employed, and selective. They arrive skeptical of recruiters and want signal that Stem Connect actually understands their craft and the market. Context: browsing on desktop or mobile, comparing this against being contacted cold on LinkedIn.
- **Employers** — founders, hiring managers, and talent leads at Canadian startups and scale-ups. They need quality hires fast and are tired of transactional, high-volume agencies. Context: evaluating whether to trust a boutique partner with a critical hire.

The job to be done: convince a discerning visitor, in seconds, that Stem Connect is the sharp, market-fluent, boutique partner — then route them to the right next step (see a role / apply, or start a search).

## Product Purpose

Stem Connect is a boutique Canadian tech recruiting agency (Toronto/GTA, expanding nationwide) specializing in Product, Design, Engineering, Data, and Go-to-Market roles. The site is the brand's primary surface: it must communicate expertise and taste, not just list jobs. Success = a qualified candidate or employer reaches out (or applies) because the site made Stem Connect feel unmistakably more credible and more considered than the alternatives.

## Brand Personality

**Sharp. High-signal. Confident.** Voice is precise and editorial — a firm that knows the market cold and doesn't pad. Restrained energy over hype: authority carried by typography, whitespace, and exactness rather than volume. Human underneath the edge (recruiting is about people), but never soft or generic. The acid-lime is a signal, used with intent — not decoration.

Three words: **precise, market-fluent, uncompromising.**

## Anti-references

Explicitly should NOT feel like:

- **A generic job board** (Indeed / LinkedIn Jobs) — dense listings, stock corporate photography, blue-and-white SaaS blandness.
- **A templated AI landing page** — tiny uppercase tracked eyebrows above every section, identical icon-card grids, gradient hero text, the hero-metric template, numbered `01/02/03` section markers.
- **A corporate consultancy** (Big-4) — muted navy, stock handshake photos, faceless jargon, stiffness.
- **Loud crypto/web3** — neon overload, glowing gradients everywhere, aggressive maximalism that undercuts trust. The lime stays precise, not radioactive.

## Design Principles

1. **Signal over noise.** Every element earns its place. If it doesn't add information or conviction, it's cut. Whitespace and restraint are the point.
2. **Typography is the identity.** Authority comes from type and hierarchy, not ornament. Big, confident, exact.
3. **The lime is a scalpel, not a floodlight.** Acid-lime marks the one thing that matters in a view — a CTA, a key metric, an active state — never everywhere at once.
4. **Show market fluency.** Copy and content should read like people who genuinely know Canadian tech hiring — specific numbers, real specialties, no fluff.
5. **Two clear paths, no confusion.** Candidates and employers each get an unambiguous route; the dual audience never muddies a given view.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- Target WCAG 2.1 AA. Body text ≥4.5:1, large text ≥3:1 against its background (verify lime and muted-gray combinations especially).
- Full `prefers-reduced-motion` support — every entrance/scroll animation has a crossfade or instant fallback.
- Keyboard-navigable throughout (Radix primitives for menus/dialogs/accordions); visible focus states.
- Don't rely on the lime accent alone to convey meaning (color-blind safety): pair with text, weight, or icon.
